The premise

You cannot throw anyone out. You cannot hurt anyone. You have to make leaving their idea.

Win

Usher every guest off the lot alive, using nothing but the house: the lights, the speakers, the fridge, the doors, and a plate of something warm in the right room at the right time.

Lose

The Suspicion Meter reaches 100 and the neighbours make the call — or you seriously injure somebody getting them out the door.

01 · The Hour

A turn isn't three actions. It's sixty minutes.

Everything the host does costs time you can picture. Plan the hour, take any of it back, then end it — and watch the house answer. The humane options are slow. The fast ones are loud. Try it.

02 · The Pull

Nobody tells you what they want. Watch who moves.

Every guest is secretly drawn to one thing — or, for some, driven off by it. You find out the way you would at a real party: switch something on and see who drifts toward it.

    04 · The Cast

    Twelve people who will not go home.

    Each one written by hand. Every character is funny in a way that is also a little sad — which is what makes you want to know them rather than just clear them.

    Hover a guest to hear them. None of them will tell you what they're drawn to — that's the game.

    05 · The Cameras

    The only way to be sure is to watch.

    The surveillance PC names one guest's secret per sitting — after you've done twelve different jobs around the house. But a monitor is a light, and it sits in the one room nobody may enter. The tool and the hazard are the same object.

    06 · Under the floorboards

    Every sprite is a string.

    No image files. Every guest, lamp and slice of pizza is authored in plain text — one character per pixel — and painted at runtime. Every guest is stitched together from hand-drawn hair, outfit and accessory masks, then rotted a little more at every stage. This picture is being drawn from the text beside it.
