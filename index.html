The premise

You cannot throw anyone out. You cannot hurt anyone. You have to make leaving their idea.

Win

Usher every guest off the lot alive, using nothing but the house: the lights, the speakers, the fridge, the doors, and a plate of something warm in the right room at the right time.

Lose

The Suspicion Meter reaches 100 and the neighbours make the call — or you seriously injure somebody getting them out the door.

12

guests, each written by hand

Every one has a secret pull, a temper, and things to say about the others.

28

rooms on three floors

A party floor, a private floor and a basement behind it all, joined by four staircases.

60

minutes in every turn

Walking, talking and switching things on all spend the same clock. Plan it, undo it, then commit.

0

people harmed

No weapons and no health bars. The house is your only tool.

The house

One long night, one whole street watching.

The party fills the ground floor. Upstairs is supposed to be private. The basement is where the food and the back way out are. Across the road, every window that lights up is a neighbour paying attention.

These show the new look, which is still being built. The playable alpha has the same house and rules in its older style.

01 · The Hour

A turn isn't three actions. It's sixty minutes.

Everything the host does costs time you can picture. Plan the hour, take any of it back, then end it — and watch the house answer. The humane options are slow. The fast ones are loud. Try it.

02 · The Pull

Nobody tells you what they want. Watch who moves.

Every guest is secretly drawn to one thing — or, for some, driven off by it. You find out the way you would at a real party: switch something on and see who drifts toward it.

    04 · The Cast

    Twelve people who will not go home.

    Each one written by hand. Every character is funny in a way that is also a little sad — which is what makes you want to know them rather than just clear them.

    Hover a guest to hear them. None of them will tell you what they're drawn to — that's the game.

    05 · The Cameras

    The only way to be sure is to watch.

    The surveillance PC names one guest's secret per sitting — after you've done twelve different jobs around the house. But a monitor is a light, and it sits in the one room nobody may enter. The tool and the hazard are the same object.

    06 · Under the floorboards

    Every sprite is a string.

    No image files. Every guest, lamp and slice of pizza is authored in plain text — one character per pixel — and painted at runtime. Every guest is stitched together from hand-drawn hair, outfit and accessory masks, then rotted a little more at every stage.     Questions
    
          
    Before you let yourself in.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            Is it free?
            
    The web alpha is free and needs no account. Pricing for the Steam release has not been decided.
    
          
    
          
    
            What do I need to play?
            
    A laptop or desktop with a current browser, a mouse and a keyboard. The alpha is not built for phones.
    
          
    
          
    
            How finished is it?
            
    The house, the guests and the rules are all there and playable. There is no win or lose screen, no sound and no saved game yet, and reloading starts the night again.
    
          
    
          
    
            When is it on Steam?
            
    No date yet. The roadmap above is the order things happen in.
    
          
    
          
    
            How do I tell you what I think?
            
    Inside the game, the Playtest feedback button collects your notes with the state of your run. Copy or download the report and send it to whoever gave you the link. Nothing is sent automatically.
    
          
    
          
    
            Is anyone actually hurt?
            
    No. You cannot attack anyone. The worst thing you can do is shove somebody, and the game treats that as the mistake it is.
